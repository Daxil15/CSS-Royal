A white rooster forages across a lawn, its head lowered nearly to the grass, caught mid-search for whatever seeds or insects might be hiding in the green. The bird's plumage is a soft cream-white, touched here and there with faint buff and amber tones along the tail and wing feathers, as though the color has been diluted by sunlight or simply chosen by genetics to be understated rather than showy. It is not the brilliant, multicolored rooster of storybooks and weathervanes; it is quieter, almost monochrome, which somehow makes the small bursts of color on its face all the more striking.
